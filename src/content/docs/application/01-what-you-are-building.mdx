Danube Cloud hosts the connectors of Costa Frames, Lindner Bikes, and many other members of the Bike Dataspace. These members do not want to work with a connector. They want their customers to get current certificates, and they want their suppliers' certificates to arrive in the systems they already use. Danube Cloud therefore builds an application on top of its platform: the supplier certificate service. In this path you are on the team that builds it.

## Two applications

| | Supplier certificate service | Battery data service |
|---|---|---|
| **Use case** | Supplier certificates | Battery passport data |

## What the platform already does

A dataspace application starts with a lot in place. The following tasks are done by platform services, and you call them through their APIs.

| Task | Done by |
|---|---|
| Hold the participant's keys and credentials and present them | Credential Service |
| Prove to a counterparty what the dataspace requires, such as membership, and check the counterparty's proof | Connector and Credential Service |
| Publish offers, answer catalog requests, negotiate contracts, manage transfers | Connector |
| Issue the access token for an agreed data flow | Siglet, the platform's token service for data planes |

Your application never handles a verifiable credential and never signs a protocol message. When a call from another company reaches your code, the platform has already established who is calling and that a contract exists.

## What remains yours

The connector settles who may talk to whom about which API. Everything after that is the use case itself.

- **The use-case API.** How a customer asks for a certificate, how the supplier answers, and how a renewed certificate is announced.
- **Decisions.** Which customer gets which certificate, by rule or after a person approved.
- **Records.** Certificates and their versions, requests, and what was shared with whom under which agreement.
- **Integration.** The connection to the document store on one side and to supplier management on the other.
- **Operations.** Retries, events, monitoring, and recovery after a failure.

This is more than three API calls, and it is where most of your time goes.

## Where the application sits

The use-case path names four building blocks per participant: connector, Credential Service, data plane, and business systems. The data plane is the block that serves the data once a contract exists.

For a use case that exchanges data through an API, the data plane has two parts. Siglet does the part that every use case needs. It takes instructions from the connector and issues an access token for each agreed flow. Your application does the part that is specific to the use case. It serves the API and checks the token on every call.

## One application, both roles

A **use-case application** implements one use case on both sides. The supplier certificate service lets Costa provide certificates and lets Lindner request them. It also lets Lindner provide its own certificates to its customers, and it lets Costa collect certificates from its aluminium suppliers.

This follows from the dataspace itself, where provider and consumer are roles in one exchange and not types of company. Build the application so that every tenant can act in both roles from the first day. The two sides share most parts: the client for the connector, the token handling, the store, and the log.

## Key takeaways

- The platform provides identity, proof of credentials, contracts, and access tokens. You call these services and do not rebuild them.
- A use-case application implements the use-case API, the decisions, the records, and the operations around them.
- For an API use case, Siglet and your application together are the data plane.
- One use-case application serves both roles, because every participant can provide and consume.
