Lindner Bikes is reviewing its suppliers and needs Costa's current certificates. Lindner is a member of the Bike Dataspace and is hosted by Danube Cloud, like Costa. Its supplier management system starts the exchange.

## Asking for the catalog

Lindner knows Costa as a supplier and knows Costa's DID. From the DID document, Lindner's connector learns where Costa's connector can be reached. It sends a **catalog request**: a question asking what Costa offers.

Costa's connector does not answer anonymous questions. Before it replies, it wants to know who is asking.

## Proving membership

The proof happens between the two Credential Services, automatically and in a few hundred milliseconds:

1. Lindner's connector attaches a short-lived token to the request. The token is signed with Lindner's key and says: "I am Lindner, and you may ask my Credential Service about my membership."
2. Costa's connector verifies the signature against Lindner's DID document.
3. Costa's connector asks Lindner's Credential Service for the membership credential.
4. Lindner's Credential Service presents the credential.
5. Costa's connector checks that the Bike Industry Association signed it, that it belongs to Lindner, and that it is still valid.

Nobody contacts the association during this exchange. Costa relies on the association's signature, which it can verify by itself.

## The catalog is personal

Costa's connector now compares Lindner's credentials with the access policy of each offer. It returns the offers that Lindner is eligible for.

Two consumers can therefore receive different catalogs from the same provider. A company that is not a member receives no offers and learns nothing about what Costa holds.

Lindner finds the offer "Company certificates of Costa Frames" together with its conditions.

## Negotiating the contract

Lindner wants the certificates and accepts the conditions. Its connector sends a **contract request** for the offer. Costa's connector checks Lindner's credentials again, this time against the contract policy, and agrees. Both sides confirm.

The result is a **contract agreement**. It records:

- which data it covers;
- which provider and which consumer concluded it;
- under which policy;
- when.

Both connectors store the agreement. If a question comes up later about what was shared and on which terms, both companies hold the same record.

For a standard offer such as this one, the negotiation is automatic and finishes in seconds. A provider can also require that a person approves each request before the agreement is concluded. That is the provider's choice, per offer.

## What did not happen

Lindner did not register in a Costa portal. Nobody at Costa received an email or created an account. Lindner did not need a personal contact at Costa, and Costa did not need to check Lindner's legitimacy by hand. The membership credential covered that.

Lindner has an agreement now. It does not have the certificates yet. The agreement is the permission to fetch them, and the next lecture covers how.

## Key takeaways

- A consumer asks a provider directly for its catalog. The provider answers according to what the consumer can prove.
- Credentials are presented from Credential Service to connector. The issuer is not involved at that moment.
- A contract negotiation ends in an agreement that both sides store.
- Routine negotiations are automatic. A provider can add manual approval where it wants it.
