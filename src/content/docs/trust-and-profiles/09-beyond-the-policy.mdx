At the end of the last lecture Lindner Bikes had a contract with Costa Frames. The association had verified the facts, Costa's condition was met, and the agreement was signed. One question was not answered by any of that: which of Costa's certificates does Lindner get? The working group has to decide how much of this question belongs to the dataspace.

:::note[In this lecture]
- What a contract settles, and what it leaves open.
- What each participant decides for itself.
- What the dataspace still has to settle for those decisions.
- What can be shown afterwards.
:::

## What a contract leaves open

The first lecture sorted trust into three layers. The lectures since then have filled two of them.

| Layer | Question | Settled by | Covered in |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facts | What has been verified about this company? | Credentials from the association | Lectures 4, 5, and 7 |
| Rules | May this company take part in this use case? | The conditions on an offer, and the signed agreement | Lectures 6 and 8 |
| Decisions | Does it get this item, now? | The participant that owns the data | This lecture |

A contract answers the second question, once, for everything that is shared under the use case. It does not answer the third, and it should not.

- **Single items.** Costa has dozens of certificates and Brandt hundreds of battery models. A condition for each would turn one contract into hundreds and would show every member what a company holds.
- **Business relations.** Which customers Costa supplies changes from week to week. Conditions that list them would have to be published again each time.
- **Judgment.** "Release audit reports only after someone has looked at the request" is a process, not a fact about the requester.

## What each participant decides

These decisions stay with the company that owns the data, and its application carries them out. Costa keeps a list of the customers it works with, and a member that is not on it gets a polite refusal. Costa also decides which certificate each customer may have, as Brandt decides which battery records a recycler receives. Some requests are answered at once, and others wait for a person.

None of this involves anyone outside the company. Costa adds a customer on Monday, and its certificates reach that customer on Monday. No offer, no credential, and no part of the profile changes.

## What the dataspace still settles

A company can decide well only if it knows whom it is dealing with. The dataspace therefore settles how verified facts reach the place where the decision is made.

The profile names the facts that an application is told about a requester. In the Bike Dataspace these are who the requester is and, for a recycler, its attested activities. Only these facts count. An application does not ask for credentials a second time, and it never relies on what a requester says about itself.

The facts also have to stay fresh. What an application is told dates from the start of a transfer, so the rule from [lecture 7](../07-credential-lifecycle/) applies here as well: no transfer runs longer than a day, and a changed or withdrawn credential takes effect within a day.

One more point belongs to the use-case owner. A refusal is a normal answer, and the description of a use case says how it is reported, so that the other side can tell "not for you" from an error.

## What can be shown afterwards

A decision that a company makes for itself still has to stand up later. Four records answer what a dispute asks, and each has an owner from an earlier lecture.

| Question | Record | Kept by |
|---|---|---|
| Why was this fact attested? | The application and the review | The association |
| What did the company agree to? | The signed agreement with its version | The association |
| On which conditions did the two connect? | The contract | Both companies |
| What was requested, released, or refused? | The log of the application | Each company |

The last record is the only one that the dataspace cannot produce for a participant. A data sharing agreement can require it.

## Key takeaways

- A contract admits a company to a use case. Which item it gets is decided by the participant that owns the data.
- Partners, items, and approvals change without touching an offer, a credential, or the profile.
- The dataspace settles which verified facts an application is told, that only those count, and how long they may be relied on.
- The association's records, the contract, and each company's own log together show afterwards what happened and why.
